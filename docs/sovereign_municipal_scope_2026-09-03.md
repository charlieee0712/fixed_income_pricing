## 2. The curve convention — decided, with the evidence

**Every bond is discounted on its own CURRENCY's curve.** This matches `zeroyield4(ccy, date)`
in the legacy system, `ZeroCurve.from_currency` in ours, and the corporate book.

The alternative — a per-country government curve for each euro sovereign — was tested and
rejected. It prices a German Bund (4.25% 2014-07-04, price 109.6202) at **+1.34 bp** on
`Germany_Yield_Curve.txt` against **−44.88 bp** on `EUR_Yield_Curve.txt`, so it is not a
cosmetic choice. Three reasons the currency curve wins:

1. **Ireland has no curve file.** A per-country rule would put 2 Irish holdings on a different
   footing from their 28 euro peers — the "two owners of one decision" shape this project has
   now closed four times.
2. It keeps sovereign and corporate paper in the same currency comparable.
3. It is what the legacy system did, so the reconciliation story stays single-track.

The per-country curve remains available and is the **cross-check**, not the basis.

### What `EUR_Yield_Curve.txt` actually is

It is a **euro-area sovereign composite, not a swap curve.** Verified two ways on 2009-03-31:

- it sits **strictly between Germany and Italy at every tenor** (0.25y through 20y);
- a debt-weighted average of the six euro-area country files reproduces it to a **mean error
  of 7 bp and a maximum of 18 bp**.

| tenor | 0.25 | 1 | 2 | 5 | 7 | 10 | 20 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Germany | 0.57 | 0.81 | 1.29 | 2.25 | 2.63 | 2.99 | 3.98 |
| **EUR (composite)** | **0.78** | **0.88** | **1.46** | **2.70** | **3.23** | **3.77** | **4.38** |
| Italy | 1.17 | 1.48 | 1.90 | 3.07 | 3.61 | 4.39 | 5.29 |

This decides what the spread column is *called*. A Bund at −44.88 bp on that curve is **rich to
the euro-area average** — relative value inside the sovereign sector. It is **not** an
asset-swap spread and must never be reported as one.

### Three meanings, carried per row

The CSV column `spread_meaning` says which applies, because the same arithmetic means three
different things:

| label | who | expect |
|---|---|---|
| `own-curve-anchor` | the issuer IS the currency's sovereign (110 holdings) | ≈ 0 — a validation anchor, not a credit signal |
| `relative-to-euro-composite` | euro-area sovereigns (30) | rich/cheap vs the euro average |
| `spread-over-government` | foreign sovereigns in USD, provinces, municipalities (14) | a genuine spread |

## 3. The price/par quotation — one identity, an explicit registry

For six holdings the custodian records **par as a count of titles**, not a currency face
amount, and quotes the price per title. The detector is the custodian's own identity, and it
is emphatically **not** the price level:

```
currency face:   BT == MV_base * fx / par * 100      148 of 154
titles of F:     BT == MV_base * fx / par              6 of 154   (ratio exactly 0.01)
```

The identity **cannot** say what `F` is — it holds for MXN (`F`=100) and BRL (`F`=1000)
alike — so `F` comes from an explicit per-currency registry, never a sniff at the price. That
is the `PAR_YIELD_UNITS` lesson applied before shipping instead of after: no threshold
separates a 916.73 per-1000 quote from a per-100 one.

Both registry values are **corroborated inside the data itself**: every MXN long description
carries the token `MXN100` and the BRL carries `BRL1000`. A test asserts that agreement, so
the registry cannot drift away from the source.

Once `F` is known: `bt_per_100 = BT / (F/100)` and `par_face = par * F`. The divisor is
computed that way round so that `F`=100 gives exactly `1.0` and the **five MXN prices pass
through bit-identically** (asserted with `==`).

| security | ccy | recorded BT | used per 100 |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| `TNTD04664855`, `TNTG412489W`, `TNTG621447U`, `TNTG746992U`, `TNTG753165U` | MXN | 99.46 – 117.79 | unchanged |
| `TNTG630781W` NOTA DO TESOURO NACIONAL 10% 2017 | BRL | **916.73** | **91.673** |

**The custodian made this same mistake.** Its own yield for the Brazilian bond is
**DI = −23.1 %**, against 6.45–8.41 % for the five Mexican bonds — computed by treating 916.73
as a per-100 price. `DI` is not a usable cross-check for that security. Our converted price
calibrates to **−57.5 bp** on the Brazilian government curve; the unconverted one gives a
spread no sovereign has ever traded at, which a test pins so the conversion cannot be dropped
as cosmetic.

## 4. Four securities read individually

| security | what it is | treatment |
|---|---|---|
| `TNTD03978845` US TREAS 12.5% 2014, callable 2009-08-15 | a genuine callable Treasury; gap 1,826 days | **BDT lattice**, Bermudan par call from the master AB date, guarded by `check_representable` |
| `TNTD03983600` TREAS BD STRIPPED CALL | call date **equals** maturity — a STRIP, not an option | `zero`. It would also survive the exercise branch, but for the wrong reason, so the zero rule claims it where the reason is true |
| `TNTD04437091` RUSSIAN FEDERATION 7.5% 2030 | description says **STEP UP**; no coupon path held | `coupon-schedule-unavailable`, custodian mark |
| `TNTG630227U` JAPAN FRN 11/2021 | floating-rate JGB; no reference rate or margin anywhere in our data | `floating-reference-unverified`, custodian mark |

On the Russian bond, three independent facts point the same way and are recorded rather than
argued: the description carries `STEP UP`; the custodian's effective duration is **4.08**
against roughly **10** for a 21-year 7.5 % bullet; and its yield of 8.76 % is inconsistent with
a bullet at 93.89. Together they say the notional amortises, which we cannot represent either.
Pricing it as a bullet would have produced a confident and wrong 21-year duration.

On the Japanese floater, the reference is the mismatch, not a missing parameter — the 15-year
JGB series resets off the **10-year JGB auction yield**, which a simple-forward FRN engine does
not represent, and the custodian's own **−0.475** duration is inconsistent with a short-rate
floater at 97.64. This is the opposite of the URS corporate floaters, where the reference
matched and only a margin was missing. Pricing it would be half-modelling.

**The callable Treasury's two columns must be read together.** Its calibrated spread is
**122 bp** with the call and **950 bp** straight-to-maturity, with an effective duration of
**0.39 y** against 4.05 y straight and a custodian figure of 1.75. A 12.5 % coupon against a
par call four months out is called with near certainty, so almost the whole gap is option
value. The 950 bp figure is **not** a sovereign credit spread and the flag text says so, so it
cannot be lifted out of the CSV and quoted.

Two features are **noted but not modelled**, and the row says so: the Illinois pension bond
documents a sinking fund from 2024 (its duration of 12.73 against the custodian's 12.51 is
consistent with the bullet we priced), and the Belvoir military housing bond may amortise
(11.02 against 11.13).

### The USD Treasury spread measures liquidity, not credit

| coupon bucket | n | median | range |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| low coupon ≤ 4.5 % (recent issues) | 17 | **2.7** | −6.6 to 20.9 |
| mid 4.5–6.5 % | 10 | 37.8 | −7.7 to 67.7 |
| high > 6.5 % (old bonds) | 6 | 54.9 | 42.8 to 69.4 |
| STRIPS | 30 | 46.9 | −8.0 to 77.7 |

Three securities maturing on **the same day**, 2019-02-15, settle the interpretation:

| | coupon | spread |
|---|---:|---:|
| the on-the-run 10-year auctioned Feb 2009 | 2.750 % | **+3.9 bp** |
| STRIPS | 0 % | +38.7 / +41.0 bp |
| an old off-the-run bond | 8.875 % | **+42.8 bp** |

Same issuer, same maturity, same day, 39 bp apart. Our USD curve is built from on-the-run
yields, so the calibrated number on a Treasury is the **off-the-run/liquidity premium**, which
in March 2009 was historically extreme. Recent issues anchor at ~3 bp; everything older is
40–55 bp cheap. This is a market feature, not a model error — but it means a USD Treasury
spread from this table must be read as a liquidity number.

classes are sovereign and sub-sovereign, a different branch of the Summary sheet.

Non-Government CMOs 264, plus ABS and CMBS — together with the derivative rows. All of it is
gated on the Bloomberg pull Mario has not yet returned, and none of it is unblocked by this
